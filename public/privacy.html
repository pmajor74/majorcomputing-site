The short version. The game collects nothing about you. It has no accounts, no sign-in and no server: everything it remembers is stored on your own device. The only component that can send anything anywhere is advertising, which stops for good once it is removed — with one small purchase — and which asks for your consent first wherever the law requires it.

Who this is from

Colored Sands of Egypt is published by Major Computing, in British Columbia, Canada. Questions about anything on this page can go to admin@majorcomputing.ca.

What the game stores, and where

Your progress through the chambers, your best results, your settings — sound, music, vibration, language — and which of the three unlocks you have bought are all kept in the app's own storage on your device. None of it is sent to us. We never see it, and we could not retrieve it if we wanted to. Deleting the app deletes all of it.

The game is fully playable with no network connection, and nothing in it waits on one.

Advertising

The game can show occasional full-screen advertisements between chambers, supplied by Google AdMob. It never interrupts a puzzle in progress, it never appears in the first eighteen chambers, and it is never attached to undo or restart — those are free and unlimited for everyone, always. Separately, when a hint or the extra vial has run out, you may choose to watch an advertisement for one instead of waiting a minute; that is your choice each time, and the wait is always there beside it.

When advertising is switched on, Google's Mobile Ads SDK operates inside the app and may collect information of its own, typically including a device advertising identifier, coarse location inferred from your IP address, and basic usage signals, in order to select, cap and measure advertisements. That collection is Google's, governed by Google's own privacy terms; we receive none of it beyond aggregate counts of how many advertisements were shown.

Advertisements in this game are requested as non-personalised, which means they are selected from the context rather than from a profile of you. The app does not ask for permission to track you across other companies' apps and websites, and it carries no code to do so.

You can remove advertising permanently with a single small purchase. There is no subscription, and nothing recurring.

Your choices about advertising

Where the law requires it — in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland — the game asks for your consent before any advertising code loads, using Google's own consent form. You can change that answer at any time from Options, under Advertising privacy options.

If you refuse consent, the game does not lock you out and does not demand payment. Nothing is taken away. In an advertisement's place you will occasionally see a card from us explaining that advertising is what pays for the game, and offering the two ways out: change your answer, or buy the game's advert-free unlock. The card can be dismissed, and the thousand chambers stay open either way.

Your device also has its own controls, which the game obeys: on Android, "Delete advertising ID" and the advertising settings in the system settings; on iPhone and iPad, "Allow Apps to Request to Track" under Privacy & Security.

Purchases

There are three purchases in the game — removing advertising, unlimited hints, and unlimited extra vials — and each is a single payment, once, forever. They are handled entirely by the App Store or Google Play. We never see your card, your billing address or your account. The app is told only whether each product has been bought, and it stores that answer on your device.

Children

The game is suitable for all ages and is not directed at children in particular. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age, because we do not collect personal information at all.

Your rights

Rights to access, correct, export or erase personal data apply to data a company holds. We hold none. If you want the game to forget everything it knows about your play, delete the app; that is the whole of it. For the advertising data described above, which is Google's rather than ours, Google's own privacy terms and the controls named in this page are the way to reach it.

Feedback you send us

The game has a Send Feedback button. It writes a message, with a picture of the screen if you choose to include one, and hands the whole thing to your own email app with our address already filled in. The game sends nothing itself. Nothing leaves your phone unless you press send in your own mail app, and if you change your mind there, nothing is kept anywhere.

An email that does arrive is ordinary correspondence. We read it to act on it, we may write back, and we keep it only as long as it is useful. It is never shared, never used for advertising, and never joined to anything else. It reaches us with whatever your mail app sends, which includes your email address, together with a few lines the game adds saying which version and which phone it came from and which chamber you were in.

Changes to this policy

If the game ever begins collecting something, this page will say so before it does, and the date at the top will change. There is no version of this document that quietly grows.
