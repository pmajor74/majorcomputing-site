How it plays

One rule. A thousand rooms.

Sand settles only on sand of the same colour, and only where there is room. That is the whole of it.

Tip a vessel into another and the top band pours across. Gather every pigment into a vessel of its own and the chamber is done: the glass breaks, the colours rise in a turning column of dust, and the stone door opens on the next room.

Then the vessels start to misbehave. One is sealed in wax until you gather the colour it names. One hides its sand under Nile silt. One pours from the bottom. One is a kiln that fires two pigments into a third. Every ten chambers or so, something new is waiting, and by the end they are all on the board at once.

No way to lose

No lives, no energy, no timers, no move limits. Undo goes back as far as you like. Stuck? Skip it. No chamber is a wall.

Rooms that change

Eighty-odd rooms under the necropolis, drawn in cinematic pixel art and lit by torch, brazier and shafts of daylight. A new one every ten chambers.

Sand you can touch

The dust of a finished chamber is a real fluid. Put a finger in it and it moves out of the way.

The kiln, chamber 99. Tap to play, with sound.

Made of real things

Fire two pigments into a third

Malachite and natron go in. Egyptian blue comes out, the way it actually did, four thousand years ago.

The fourteen colours are the ones Egyptian painters ground, every layer of sand wears its own hieroglyph, and a blended pigment shows the grains of both halves it is made of. It is a puzzle about sand, so the sand had to be right.

One playlist runs under the whole game, oud and ney and qanun, crossfading song to song and never restarting for a chamber. It is a calm game. It stays calm.

Fair play

The fair one

Sorting puzzles stopped being fair somewhere along the way: twelve dollars to remove the adverts, and they still charge you for undo. This one was built to be the other thing, and to say so before you install it.

  • Undo, restart and skip. Free, unlimited, forever.
  • All thousand chambers. No pack held back, no account, no server, works offline.
  • Remove the adverts, $2.99, once. Or watch adverts until they have paid for themselves, and they stop for good.
  • Unlimited hints and the extra vial, $1.29, once. Or three of each free, another every minute you wait, one for any advert you choose to watch.
  • Never sold: coins, gems, lives, energy, subscriptions, battle passes, or an offer at the moment you fail, because there is no failing.
  • Adverts come between chambers only, never over a puzzle, never in the first eighteen.
